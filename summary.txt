World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Russia–Ukraine talks on post-war security in Abu Dhabi ended without a breakthrough after a second day of US-mediated negotiations (BBC), and world leaders began speaking at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey (AFP).

In the United States, Democrat Scott Colom won Mississippi's Senate seat, CNN projected, unseating Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith to become the state's first Black senator since 1875. A bid to impeach Pete Hegseth failed after some Democrats broke ranks (WSJ).

Iranian forces attacked shipping-navigation systems off Oman's Didamar island, sources told Reuters.

Across Europe, more than 6,000 Italian schools were evacuated over hoax bomb threats, echoed in Britain and Ireland (BBC; Reuters). Storm Charles shut about 8,000 Portuguese schools and cancelled Web Summit's second day in Lisbon (AP). Germany approved a 24-terawatt-hour gas reserve and said its army would drop Chinese-made drones (AFP; AP).

In Latin America, a former Red Brigades member fled house arrest in Buenos Aires while awaiting extradition (AFP); Brazil fined Meta 7.5 million reais (US$1.34 million) over its presidential election (Le Monde); and Pope Leo XIV arrived in Peru, which unveiled a 14-metre statue of him (IJR).

In Africa, Kenya's President William Ruto signed a deal under which Sudan lifts its 20-month ban on Kenyan tea imports.

In Asia, ten police were killed in an ambush in Pakistan's Kohat district (AP), and three paramilitaries were wounded in firing in India's Jammu and Kashmir (The Hindu).

Researchers said a Kazakhstan-linked group, Silent Lynx, used an autonomous agent to breach a Tajik government database and steal over 2,000 documents (Reuters).

Elsewhere, the liner SS United States was scuttled off Florida as an artificial reef, and Russia's ambassador to the US, Alexander Darchiev, died of a chronic illness (Reuters).
